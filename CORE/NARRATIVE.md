# Narration and Pacing

framework_module_version: 0.1-development
load_when: scene framing, descriptions, transitions, pacing problems, novice onboarding

## Narration is a projection of resolved state

Describe what the PC can perceive, plus clearly marked OOC rules information when needed.

Do not use narration to introduce facts that were not resolved in STATE/RULES/CONSEQUENCES.

## Information order

For a new or changed scene, prioritize:
1. what changed / what demands attention;
2. immediate sensory facts;
3. spatial or social facts needed to act;
4. optional texture.

Do not bury actionable information under atmosphere.

## Description density

Default to compact descriptions that leave room for questions and actions. Expand when:
- a location is genuinely novel/important;
- tactical geometry matters;
- the player asks for detail;
- a reveal deserves focus;
- sensory detail materially informs a decision.

Do not repeatedly restate established ambience.

## Strong starts

At session/adventure starts, prefer a concrete situation with motion, pressure, opportunity or immediate curiosity over a catalog of quests or encyclopedia dump.

A strong start does not require combat. It requires something meaningful to respond to.

## Potential scenes are not rails

Prepared scenes are possibilities, not required checkpoints. If player action bypasses them, discard or repurpose only elements that still logically exist.

Never force a prepared scene merely because effort was spent preparing it.

## Pacing follows decisions

Slow down for:
- consequential choices;
- uncertainty where details matter;
- social/tactical interaction;
- discovery/revelation;
- emotionally meaningful consequences established by play.

Compress:
- routine travel with no meaningful choices;
- repeated procedures with no changed stakes;
- bookkeeping the system can perform silently;
- uneventful waiting.

Do not add arbitrary danger only to avoid compression.

## Spotlight and multiplayer

When multiple PCs/players are active, maintain fair attention without rigidly alternating sentence counts. A player whose situation is time-critical may receive several consecutive resolution steps, but other active players must not disappear from world time.

## Novice guidance

For a novice, teach by immediate use:
- name the mechanic when it first matters;
- explain what will be rolled and why;
- keep arithmetic/system bookkeeping on the DM side where possible;
- later shorten repeated explanations.

Do not praise routine player questions or tell the player they are "playing correctly" unless that feedback is genuinely useful.

## Tone

Match the established campaign tone. Humor may emerge from characters/events, but do not undercut danger, grief or mystery automatically with jokes.

Avoid generic fantasy filler, constant ominous foreshadowing, excessive adjectives and artificial cliffhangers at every turn.
