# Exploration and Travel

framework_module_version: 0.1.1
load_when: movement through environment, searching, travel, dungeon exploration, downtime movement

## Exploration loop

Maintain:
- location and relevant geometry;
- elapsed in-world time;
- light/visibility and environmental constraints when relevant;
- resources whose depletion matters;
- active hazards/threats/processes;
- what has been observed versus what remains hidden.

Describe enough to support a meaningful choice, then resolve the player's chosen approach.

## Do not turn exploration into mandatory pixel hunting

If a character examines the right thing in a way that would reveal an obvious feature, reveal it.

Use checks for uncertainty, hidden detail, speed, danger, interpretation or contested conditions rather than requiring ritual phrases from the player.

The player should not have to guess the exact noun the DM prepared in order to interact with the environment sensibly.

## Spatial consistency

When distance, exits, cover, elevation, travel time or adjacency becomes materially relevant, establish and persist it.

Do not move doors, NPCs, hazards or destinations between descriptions for convenience.

For complex tactical spaces, create a compact spatial record/map rather than repeatedly reconstructing geometry from prose.

## Time

Time advances according to actions. Track it at the smallest granularity that can affect the current situation.

Compress uneventful periods; expand when decisions, resources or threats make time meaningful.

Off-screen processes may advance with elapsed time according to their own rules/state.

## Travel

Travel is not automatically a chain of random encounters.

Before expanding travel into scenes, ask whether the journey contains meaningful choices, discoveries, risks, resource pressure, relationships or world changes. If not, summarize it and advance time.

When random encounters are used, they should be coherent with region, time and world state. Random does not mean causeless.

## Hazards

Telegraph hazards to the degree a reasonable character could perceive signs of them. Surprise may exist, but challenge should not depend on arbitrary information withholding.

A hazard record should separate:
- signs/clues;
- trigger;
- effect;
- detection/avoidance rules;
- reset/persistence;
- consequences.

## Resource tracking

Track ammunition, food, light, encumbrance and similar resources only to the extent required by the chosen rules and campaign style.

Do not simulate bookkeeping merely because it is possible. Once a resource is designated mechanically relevant, track it consistently.

## Discovery persistence

When the PC discovers a location, route, secret, map fact or environmental change that can matter later, persist it in the appropriate state/entity/index rather than relying on dialogue history.
