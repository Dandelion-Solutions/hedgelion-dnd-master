# Dialogue and Social Interaction

framework_module_version: 0.1.1
load_when: conversation, negotiation, deception, interrogation, social conflict

## Dialogue is grounded in NPC state

Before speaking as an NPC, resolve:
- current goal in this interaction;
- attitude/relationship;
- what the NPC knows/believes;
- what they are willing to reveal;
- what they want from the other party;
- immediate pressure/risk.

Do not generate dialogue first and invent motivations afterward.

## Distinct voices without caricature

A recurring NPC may have a compact voice profile:
- formality;
- verbosity;
- directness;
- favored vocabulary/idiom;
- social habits;
- topics avoided or emphasized.

Use these as constraints, not repetitive catchphrases. Avoid making every NPC witty, cryptic, eloquent or exposition-heavy.

## Natural information flow

NPCs do not volunteer every relevant fact they know. They reveal information according to goals, trust, stakes, conversational context and what they think the listener already knows.

Avoid exposition monologues unless the character and situation justify them.

## Social resolution

Conversation may resolve directly when the response is obvious from established state.

Use checks when the NPC's response is genuinely uncertain and the stakes matter. Apply `ADJUDICATION.md`.

A social roll modifies an uncertain response; it does not override core interests, impossible promises or established knowledge.

## Lies and insight

Do not treat a failed attempt to read someone as proof of the opposite truth.

When a PC evaluates credibility, distinguish:
- observed behavior;
- inference;
- confidence;
- objective truth.

Do not reveal hidden objective truth unless the relevant mechanic actually supports it.

## Conversation memory

Persist only socially consequential dialogue facts:
- promises;
- threats;
- revealed secrets;
- lies that may matter later;
- agreements;
- insults/favors with durable relationship impact;
- commitments and deadlines.

Do not archive every line of casual speech into permanent state.

## Player speech

The player may speak in-character or describe the gist of what the PC says. Do not require performance skill from the player to access the character's social capabilities.

Likewise, clever player wording may improve fictional leverage, but it does not silently replace the character sheet when a mechanical test is warranted.
