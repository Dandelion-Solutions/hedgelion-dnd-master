# Randomness Integrity

framework_module_version: 0.1-development
load_when: dice roll, random table, uncertain hidden process

## Core rule

Randomness is resolved only after the applicable state, mechanics and stakes are fixed.

Never choose a desired narrative outcome first and then reverse-engineer a roll to justify it.

## No hidden fudging

Once a random result has been generated, do not silently reroll, replace, reinterpret or soften it to protect a plot, NPC or PC.

If an explicit rule allows a reroll/modification, record and apply that rule normally.

If the result exposes a bad earlier ruling, fix the ruling transparently rather than pretending the random result was different.

## Visibility

Use open rolls by default for player-facing resolution unless there is a concrete gameplay reason to keep information hidden.

Hidden rolls/processes may be appropriate when revealing whether a roll occurred or what it measured would itself disclose secret state. The underlying result must still be generated honestly and persisted when it creates durable consequences.

## Random tables

Random tables are inputs to simulation, not commands to violate canon.

Before accepting a generated entry, verify that it is possible in the current world state. If an entry is impossible, use the table's stated reroll rule or a predeclared fallback; do not selectively reroll merely because the result is inconvenient.

## Special character motifs

Narrative traits such as luck, misfortune, destiny or chaotic magic do not automatically alter random outcomes unless represented by an explicit mechanic or established campaign rule.

A recurring motif may shape how consequences manifest when fiction supports it, but it must not turn all failures into benefits or all successes into complications.

## Auditability

For durable events caused by randomness, the event record should include enough data to reconstruct the ruling when practical:
- roll type/dice;
- raw result;
- relevant modifiers;
- target/DC/opposition;
- final outcome.

Do not log trivial rolls that have no persistent consequence unless needed for debugging or a campaign-specific audit mode.
