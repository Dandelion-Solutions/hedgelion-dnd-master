# Lore and History Discipline

framework_module_version: 0.1.1
load_when: creating history/culture, revealing lore, ancient sites, religion, institutions, disputed history

## Lore exists to make the present world specific

History matters when it leaves traces in current geography, institutions, customs, conflicts, language, ruins, laws, technology/magic, family claims, religion or material culture.

Prefer a few consequential historical facts over a huge chronology with no effect on play.

## Separate truth from accounts

Store separately when relevant:
- objective historical fact;
- what a culture officially teaches;
- what a faction claims;
- what an NPC believes;
- rumor, myth or propaganda;
- what a PC has actually learned.

Two contradictory legends do not require objective history to be contradictory. An unreliable source can be wrong without the DM changing canon.

## Build history causally

When establishing a new historical fact, check compatibility with:
- established chronology;
- geography and travel;
- factions and political power;
- economy/resources;
- religion/cosmology adopted by the campaign;
- magic/technology level;
- existing ruins, artifacts and institutions;
- lifespans and ancestry when materially relevant.

A major historical claim should explain or constrain something in the present.

## Reveal lore through play

Avoid encyclopedic monologues.

Break important lore into small revelations that can appear through multiple natural channels: architecture, inscriptions, rituals, laws, songs, artifacts, NPC testimony, documents, ruins, consequences or direct research.

If a historical conclusion is required for progress, use redundant evidence rather than one exposition dump or one fragile check.

Give significance before payoff when practical: a discovery is stronger when the players already understand why it matters.

## Exposition drip, not exposition flood

For complex lore:
1. identify the important conclusion;
2. split it into digestible revelations;
3. reveal pieces when fiction provides a plausible channel;
4. revisit through different contexts when useful;
5. do not force every prepared revelation into play.

Lore should accumulate recognition, not homework.

## Spiral depth

Develop the history immediately around the PCs more deeply than remote continents and forgotten millennia.

It is valid for distant history to remain undefined. Do not invent a complete cosmology merely because one inscription mentions an old god.

When play reaches a boundary, expand the world consistently from what is already known.

## Attention is not destiny

A player asking repeatedly about a statue, family name or old battle does not retroactively make it central to a conspiracy or prophecy.

Interest may justify doing more preparation around that subject. New importance must arise through established facts or later causal interaction.

## Motifs without universal conspiracy

Recurring names, symbols, cultural echoes and historical parallels can create depth. Do not connect every recurrence to the same hidden mastermind.

Coincidence, imitation, common culture and independent causes are all legitimate world explanations.

## Canonization threshold

Persist lore when future consistency could depend on it: dates/order, rulers, wars, institutions, origin of a significant item/site, religious doctrine, treaty, disaster, lineage, cultural taboo or other consequential fact.

Disposable atmospheric color need not become a permanent lore record unless later play makes it important.

Never rewrite established history to make a new twist more elegant. If a new discovery changes what characters believe about history, update their knowledge — not objective history unless the prior objective record was actually erroneous.
