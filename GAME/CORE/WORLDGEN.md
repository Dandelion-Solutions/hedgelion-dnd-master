# World Generation and Expansion

framework_module_version: 0.2.0
load_when: new campaign, new region, expanding undefined world, creating local context

## Build only the reachable horizon

At campaign creation, define only what is needed to make the first meaningful decisions coherent: campaign premise/tone, starting area, immediately relevant powers/problems, and facts required by the PC concept.

Do not pre-generate a continent-sized encyclopedia before play.

Undefined world is allowed. Mark it undefined rather than pretending a hidden answer already exists.

## Establish facts before using them

When a new detail becomes necessary, derive it from existing geography, factions, economy, culture, magic and causality. Persist only details likely to matter later.

Player curiosity can justify looking more closely at the world; it does not require every answer to be extraordinary or plot-relevant.

## Source-backed enrichment

External research is allowed and often useful during worldbuilding/prep when no unresolved player action is waiting.

Use `PLAY_POLICY.md` research modes:
- official D&D/SRD/selected-setting sources for authoritative published facts;
- reputable wikis as secondary lore/navigation references;
- forums, blogs and community material as inspiration rather than automatic canon.

Research a concrete worldbuilding question, not a whole setting. Batch the lookup, stop when the current horizon is sufficiently rich, and distill only adopted facts/ideas into campaign records.

Do not make the first scene wait for a broad lore crawl. If a detail is not needed yet, leave it undefined and research it later at a prep boundary.

## D&D lore fidelity

Respect `CONFIG.play_style.dnd_lore_fidelity` when deciding how strongly external D&D lore constrains creation.

- Low values: use D&D lore/terminology freely as inspiration; original reinterpretation is welcome while mechanics remain D&D-consistent.
- Middle values: preserve recognizable D&D concepts and useful published lore, but prioritize coherent established campaign fiction over minor source minutiae.
- High values: for an adopted published setting/source canon, verify important setting facts and terminology more carefully and treat them as strong constraints unless campaign canon explicitly diverges.

This setting NEVER changes dice math, PC capabilities, resource costs or other adopted mechanics.

Campaign canon already established in storage outranks later external research unless an explicit correction/migration is authorized.

## Local consistency

New locations and institutions should have plausible relationships to nearby settlements, routes, resources, authority and threats. Travel distances and connections become canonical once materially established.

## Scale expansion

Expand outward when play reaches or investigates a boundary:
local scene -> settlement/dungeon -> local region -> neighboring powers -> wider world.

Do not reveal higher-scale lore simply because it exists in prep.

## Setting choices

A campaign may use an original world, SRD-compatible generic fantasy, a remixed published setting, or an explicitly source-faithful published setting.

Do not assume Forgotten Realms or another published setting merely because D&D mechanics are used. The selected setting and lore-fidelity preference determine how external source canon is treated.

## World change

The world is mutable through events. Destruction, construction, political shifts, economic shocks and discoveries update canonical records rather than being treated as temporary narration.
